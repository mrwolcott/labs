How to use this

Cysts (setup): spread your cysts on a light surface, photograph straight down, and tap each one. Adjust until the total reaches your target, then add exactly that many to the jar.

Nauplii · photo: pour the whole jar into a wide shallow tray so the shrimp spread into a thin single layer, photograph straight down, and tap each swimming shrimp. If one photo cannot show the whole tray clearly, photograph it in halves and use Save this photo & start another so the halves add together.

Nauplii · video (motion): brace your phone, let the poured tray settle for about twenty seconds so the water stops drifting, then record about five seconds straight down. The tool keeps the objects that swim and ignores the ones that stay still, which are cysts, empty shells, dead shrimp, and yeast, then marks the swimmers for you to check the same way. Video is the more reliable count when the phone is steady and the water has settled. If a clip will not load, it is almost always an iPhone recording in HEVC: set Settings, Camera, Formats to Most Compatible and record it again. This mode is a first prototype and its sensitivity will be tuned once real footage exists.

The assist: tap five to ten specimens yourself first, then press Assist: find the rest. The tool learns their color and size from your taps and marks others like them in teal. Teal marks are guesses, so check every one: tap a wrong mark to remove it, tap a missed shrimp to add it. The final number is yours, not the tool's.

  • Tap an empty spot to add a mark. Tap a mark to remove it.
  • Drag to move the photo. Pinch, scroll, or use Zoom to get in close on crowded spots.
  • Good light and a plain background make both your count and the assist far more reliable.

This tool only counts. It does not calculate percentages or tell you whether your number is right. Enter the total in your lab report yourself.
